03 · Opportunity sizing · pricing change

What would a minimum floor price earn?

New floor = the higher of today's floor and X. Impressions that paid less than X are at risk: some still sell at X, the rest are lost. Nobody knows that share yet, so the model shows the range and the break-even point. Pick a scenario.

Assumes bids don't change and ignores long-run effects (advertisers cutting budgets). That is why the recommendation is to A/B test a floor, which measures the "still sell" share directly. Model: docs/adr/0007.

Limits and sources

  • Not Swiggy data. Public datasets analysed the way a marketplace ads team would. No Swiggy users or numbers.
  • Fill rate and ROAS are not computable: no unfilled ad requests and no order values in any public log. Win rate and CPA are shown instead, labelled as stand-ins.
  • iPinYou bids came from a fixed high-price strategy, so bids aren't real valuations; only prices paid are used for sizing.
  • iPinYou: RTB logs, season 3 (19–28 Oct 2013), RMB fen per 1,000 impressions; Zhang et al. 2014. Criteo Attribution and Criteo Uplift v2.1: Criteo AI Lab, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.
  • Every number on this page is read from data.js, generated by the project's SQL; the same numbers feed the Excel model and the memo.
